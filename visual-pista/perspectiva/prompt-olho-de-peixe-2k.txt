Lens and framing: true equidistant fisheye, like @Image 5. The center of the frame is the zenith, straight overhead. The edge of the fisheye circle is the open horizon all around: distant hazy plains of pale sand, not a rim, not a hole, not a tunnel. The direction of travel is the right edge of the frame (the horizon ahead) and the left edge is the horizon behind.

Camera motion, very important: the camera only moves straight forward, toward the right edge of the frame, at one constant slow speed. It never spins, never rotates around the center of the image, never turns, tilts or rolls. Because we move forward, everything flows across the frame from right to left: new statues, floating stones and crystals rise from the horizon at the right edge, grow larger as we approach, pass overhead through the center of the frame with strong parallax (closer things move faster), and sink toward the horizon at the left edge.

The world: a warm gradient sky, deep dark red overhead, through red and orange, to soft magenta and pink haze near the horizon, with a few tiny stars.
