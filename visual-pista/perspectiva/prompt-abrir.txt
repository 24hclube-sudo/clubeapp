Locked-off tripod shot from the dance floor: the real camera never moves, pans or zooms, and the room never changes: the dark concrete walls, the two speaker boxes, the stone DJ booth and the glossy floor stay exactly as in the first frame. Empty club, no people.

Only the two LED screens change: the long LED ceiling and the LED wall above the DJ booth. Together they act as ONE continuous anamorphic screen folded at the far end. Everything on them is drawn in the room's own perspective and aligned to the screen edges, so from this exact viewpoint it reads as real 3D depth beyond the ceiling and the wall. The screen edges always stay sharp and straight.

0 to 2 seconds: the screens show the flat 2D image of the first frame: the silver pixelated striding woman with sunglasses, streaming hair and a raised cocktail glass, on a flat red background.
2 to 4 seconds: the flat image cracks along its pixel grid into thousands of small square tiles; the tiles flip like blades and fall away into depth, and the ceiling and the wall open up like a portal.
4 to 10 seconds: behind them is a vast three-dimensional world seen through the opening, in strong one-point perspective that continues the lines of the room: the CLUBE universe. A deep warm gradient sky from dark red to orange to soft magenta; colossal statues of the same silver pixelated figures (the striding woman, a dancer, a DJ holding a vinyl record) standing like monuments; floating slabs of rough beige stone like the DJ booth; glowing violet and magenta crystals made of tiny pixels; drifting haze and fine silver particles. The view inside the screens glides slowly forward and upward into this world, so the statues and stones pass overhead with real parallax, as if we were flying into it through the ceiling.
10 to 12 seconds: the tiles fly back and lock into place, closing the portal, and the screens return to exactly the same flat image of the striding woman on red as in the first frame.

The changing light of the screens tints the walls, the booth and the reflections on the floor. No text, no logos, no people in the room.

Sound: a deep cinematic whoosh as the screens open, an airy shimmering pad during the flight, a soft click of tiles locking at the end.
